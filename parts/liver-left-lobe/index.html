Liver - left lobe

Lobus hepatis sinister

DigestiveOrgansAbdomen

View in 3D

What it is
Thinner, flattened lobe to the left of the falciform ligament, lying over the front of the stomach and reaching into the left upper abdomen under the heart.
Function
Hepatocytes make bile, process nutrients arriving in the portal vein, store glycogen and vitamins, build plasma proteins such as albumin and clotting factors, and detoxify drugs and ammonia.
Size
About 2-3 cm thick, reaching about 8 cm left of the midline.
Clinical note
Its left lateral segments (II and III) are the part most often donated by a living adult for a liver transplant into a child.
