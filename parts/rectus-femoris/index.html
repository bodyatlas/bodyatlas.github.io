Rectus femoris

What it is
The only quadriceps head that crosses the hip: a spindle-shaped, bipennate muscle running straight down the front of the thigh from the anterior inferior iliac spine (straight head) and the rim above the acetabulum (reflected head) to the quadriceps tendon, patella and, via the patellar ligament, the tibial tuberosity.
Function
Extends the knee and flexes the hip - the main kicking muscle.
Size
About 35-40 cm long; belly up to 5 cm wide and 3 cm thick.
Clinical note
The most frequently strained quadriceps muscle (sprinting, kicking) because it crosses two joints; in adolescents its origin can avulse from the AIIS.

Nearby muscular structures Adductor brevis, Adductor longus, Adductor magnus, Biceps femoris (long head), Gracilis, Semimembranosus, Semitendinosus, Vastus intermedius, Vastus lateralis, Vastus medialis
