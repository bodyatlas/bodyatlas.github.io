Ear canal

Meatus acusticus externus

Sensory (eye & ear)OrgansHeadLeft and right

View left in 3D View right in 3D

What it is
The external acoustic meatus: a slightly S-shaped tube from the concha to the eardrum. Its outer third is cartilage carrying hairs, sebaceous and ceruminous (wax) glands; the inner two-thirds run through the temporal bone under thin, hairless skin.
Function
Conducts airborne sound to the eardrum and, by its length, resonates at about 3 kHz to boost speech-range frequencies by 10-15 dB.
Size
About 25 mm long and 7 mm in diameter, narrowest at the bony isthmus.
Clinical note
Because the canal curves, clinicians pull the auricle up and back to straighten it before looking in with an otoscope (down and back in infants).
