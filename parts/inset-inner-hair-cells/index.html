Inner hair cells (magnified)

Cellulae sensoriae internae

Sensory (eye & ear)OrgansWhole body

View in 3D

What it is
A single row of flask-shaped sensory cells, about 3,500 per ear, each crowned by a shallow line of stereocilia in three rows of graded height. Every inner hair cell is contacted by 10-20 afferent nerve fibres.
Function
The true sound receptors: bending of the stereocilia opens ion channels, depolarises the cell and releases glutamate onto cochlear-nerve endings.
Size
Real length about 35 micrometres; stereocilia 2-8 micrometres tall.
Clinical note
Loss of inner hair cells causes permanent deafness because mammalian hair cells do not regenerate.
