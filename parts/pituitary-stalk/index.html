Pituitary stalk

Infundibulum hypophysis

Endocrine & glandsGlandsHead

View in 3D

What it is
A thin funnel of nerve fibres and portal blood vessels that hangs the pituitary from the floor of the hypothalamus (the median eminence), passing through a hole in the diaphragma sellae.
Function
Carries hypothalamic axons to the posterior lobe and the hypophyseal portal veins that deliver releasing hormones to the anterior lobe.
Size
About 8–10 mm long, 2–3 mm in diameter
Clinical note
Anything that compresses the stalk (tumour, surgery, head injury) interrupts the dopamine brake on prolactin: prolactin rises while every other anterior hormone falls — the "stalk effect".
