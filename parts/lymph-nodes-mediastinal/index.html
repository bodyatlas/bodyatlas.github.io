Mediastinal lymph nodes

Nodi lymphoidei mediastinales

Lymphatic & immuneLymphaticsThorax

View in 3D

What it is
Nodes clustered around the windpipe and its fork in the middle of the chest: right and left paratracheal chains, tracheobronchial nodes at the carina, subcarinal nodes beneath it and prevascular nodes in front of the great vessels behind the thymus. Each node is a bean-shaped filter 5-15 mm long with a fibrous capsule, an outer cortex of B-cell follicles, a T-cell paracortex and an inner medulla. Several afferent lymphatics enter its convex surface; one or two efferents leave at the hilum.
Function
Receive lymph from the lungs (via the hilar nodes), heart, oesophagus and thymus and pass it to the bronchomediastinal trunks.
Size
About 20-40 nodes of 5-15 mm
Clinical note
Spread of lung cancer to these nodes (N2 disease) usually rules out simple surgery. Symmetrical enlargement of hilar and mediastinal nodes on a chest X-ray is the classic picture of sarcoidosis.
